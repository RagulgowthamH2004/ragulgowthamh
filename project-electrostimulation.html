Potential of electrostimulation in enhancing growth and yield of Amaranthus spp. in controlled environment agriculture

Type B.Tech. research projectInstitution AEC&RI, TNAU, CoimbatoreRole Project team member / student researcherField Energy and Environmental Engineering

The idea

Low-intensity direct current applied to the root zone has a long and uneven literature behind it: promising results, inconsistent reporting, and rarely enough methodological detail for anyone to repeat the experiment. This project tested the intervention on Amaranthus spp. under controlled environment agriculture conditions, sitting deliberately at the intersection of electrical engineering, plant physiology and sustainable agriculture.

The methodological argument

The treatment was characterised by current density in A m⁻², not by raw voltage or current. This is the part of the work I would defend hardest. A voltage figure is meaningless without the electrode geometry it was applied through; a current figure is meaningless without the area it passed into. Expressing the dose as current density makes it reproducible across replicates and comparable against other rigs — which is precisely what the existing literature mostly does not allow.

Design and instrumentation

  • Electrodes: silver, positioned in the root zone.
  • Supply and measurement: regulated DC supply, milliammeter and multimeter.
  • Scheduling: treatment applied across defined crop-growth stages rather than continuously.
  • Controls: matched control and treated groups.
  • Response measured: shoot and root length and biomass, Leaf Area Index, and chlorophyll a, b and total chlorophyll, evaluated statistically.

The asymmetry is the interesting result. Root biomass responded far more strongly than shoot biomass, which is what you would expect if the stimulus acts locally at the root zone rather than through a whole-plant pathway — and it points at where the mechanism should be investigated next.

Where it leads

Controlled environment agriculture is energy-intensive by construction, so any intervention has to justify itself against the electrical load it adds. Expressing dose as current density is what makes that accounting possible: you cannot weigh a yield gain against an energy cost until the dose is stated in units that carry across systems.
