Expertise areas

Two subjects I follow closely alongside my environmental management, ESG and research work. They sit next to each other in textiles and are often mentioned in the same breath, but they do different jobs — one certifies, the other informs — so they are set out separately below.

OEKO-TEX® Digital Product Passport
Expertise area

OEKO-TEX®

Textile Product Safety, Chemical Compliance & Sustainable Manufacturing

OEKO-TEX® is an international testing, certification and labelling system for textiles and leather. It verifies that articles have been tested against a defined list of harmful substances, and — depending on the standard applied — that they were made in facilities assessed for environmental performance and socially responsible working conditions. My interest here is the mechanics of it: how a restricted substance list becomes a test plan, how a test plan becomes an audit trail, and where that chain tends to break between the laboratory report and the dye house floor.

Standard

OEKO-TEX® STANDARD 100

Article-level testing against harmful substances across every component of a textile — fabric, thread, buttons, prints and coatings. Limit values are set by product class, with the strictest applied to articles for babies and small children, on the basis that skin contact and exposure differ by end use.

Standard

OEKO-TEX® MADE IN GREEN

A traceable product label that goes beyond the article itself to the conditions of its manufacture: tested for harmful substances, produced in environmentally responsible facilities, and in safe, socially responsible workplaces. Each label carries an ID allowing the production stages to be traced back.

Focus areas

Textile chemical safety

Substance behaviour across dyeing, finishing and coating, and the exposure pathways that make a substance a concern in a worn product.

Restricted substances & compliance

Reading RSL and MRSL requirements against buyer codes of conduct, and translating them into practical controls at supplier level.

Harmful substance testing

Test planning, sampling logic and interpretation of laboratory reports — including what a passing result does and does not cover.

Sustainable textile manufacturing

Water, effluent, energy and chemical management in wet processing, and the operational changes that actually move the numbers.

Certification & verification

How conformity is demonstrated, maintained and re-verified over a certificate cycle rather than at a single point in time.

Supply-chain transparency

Mapping tiers beyond the first, and the documentation needed before a claim about an upstream stage can be relied on.

Environmental & social considerations

Facility-level environmental performance and working conditions, connected to my SEDEX/SMETA and ETI Base Code experience.

Expertise area

Digital Product Passport (DPP)

Digital Traceability, Product Transparency & Circularity for Textiles

A Digital Product Passport is a structured digital record attached to a physical product, carrying information about its materials, origin, manufacture, sustainability characteristics and end-of-life handling. Under emerging EU product and sustainability requirements, textiles are among the first categories in scope. It is an information framework, not a certification — it does not judge a product, it makes what is known about the product legible to whoever holds it next: a customer, a repairer, a sorter, a recycler.

Focus areas

Product identification

Unique identifiers that persist beyond the point of sale, so a record stays attached to a specific article rather than a product line.

Material & fibre composition

Composition data at a resolution useful to sorters and recyclers, including blends, finishes and components that affect recyclability.

Supply-chain data

Stage-by-stage origin and processing information, and the supplier integration work needed to collect it reliably.

Manufacturing information

Process, facility and treatment data captured where it is generated, rather than reconstructed afterwards from invoices.

Sustainability data

Environmental indicators carried with the product — a natural join with life cycle assessment, which I have worked on directly.

Product lifecycle information

What happens to the article after purchase: durability, care, and the events worth recording over a working life.

Repair, reuse & recycling

Disassembly and repair guidance, and the end-of-life routing information a sorting facility actually needs.

Circular economy

How reliable product data changes what is economically recoverable, and where missing data forces material downcycling.

Digital product identity

QR codes and data carriers as the access point to the record, and questions of who may read which layer of it.

DPP data collection

Practical collection and supplier onboarding — the part where most implementations stall.

EU requirements

Following the developing EU sustainability and product-information framework as it applies to textiles and apparel.

Two different things

OEKO-TEX®

Product safety & certification

A testing and certification system. It assesses an article against defined criteria for harmful substances and, under MADE IN GREEN, the conditions of its production — then issues a verifiable label.

Digital Product Passport

Digital product information & traceability

A structured digital record carrying product information across the lifecycle. It discloses and transports data; it does not test, certify or assess conformity.

These are complementary, not alternatives. A certification result is one of the things a passport can carry — but a passport is not a certification, and neither replaces the other.
