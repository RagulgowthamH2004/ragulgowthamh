Monsoonal dynamics and rainfall extremes in Salem: insights from a climate variability perspective

Journal Int. J. Environment and Climate Change 15(7)Year 2025Role First authorPublished 27 June 2025

The question

Planning documents tend to quote a seasonal average and move on. But an average is a poor description of a monsoon: what determines whether a season is workable is the distribution — how the rain arrives, how far the extremes sit from the mean, and how reliably the pattern repeats. This paper examines monsoonal behaviour and rainfall extremes at Salem, Tamil Nadu, through a climate variability lens rather than a single summary statistic.

Approach

The analysis reads the rainfall record as a variable system rather than a fixed climatology, characterising monsoonal dynamics at the district scale and situating the extremes within that variability. The framing is deliberate: treating an unusual year as an anomaly assumes a stable baseline, and that assumption is exactly what a variability perspective declines to make.

Why it matters

Salem sits in a part of Tamil Nadu where agricultural decisions are made against monsoon expectation. When the expectation is drawn from a mean that no longer describes the distribution, the resulting decisions are not merely imprecise — they are systematically miscalibrated in one direction. This work is the district-scale groundwork behind the evapotranspiration and ENSO–IOD studies, which approach the same catchment from the water-demand and the ocean-forcing side respectively.

The full methodology, data treatment and results are set out in the published paper.
