ENSO–IOD coupling and monsoon response in the Indian Ocean basin during 2014–2025

Publication Agriculture & Food: e-Newsletter 8(2), 288–290Year 2026Role First authorArticle ID 72788 · ISSN 2581-8317

The subject

The El Niño–Southern Oscillation and the Indian Ocean Dipole are usually taught separately and then, in practice, encountered together. Their phases interact, and the monsoon response depends on the combination rather than on either index read alone. This article synthesises how those phases evolved across an eleven-year window and sets that evolution against observed monsoon rainfall departures.

Indices used

  • Niño-3.4 (Oceanic Niño Index) — ENSO phase and intensity.
  • Dipole Mode Index — IOD phase across the Indian Ocean basin.
  • Satellite sea surface temperatures — the observational field underlying both.
  • Monsoon rainfall departures — the response the indices are being read against.

Why it matters

Seasonal expectation in much of southern India is built on ENSO signals that are widely reported and easy to obtain. Reading them without the dipole is a known way to be confidently wrong. The value of a synthesis like this is not prediction — it is making the coupling legible to people who have to make a planting or scheduling decision before the season resolves.

Period covered: 2014 to 2025.
